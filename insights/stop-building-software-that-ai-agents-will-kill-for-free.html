Stop Building Software That AI Agents Will Kill for Free

If your app's core value is simply connecting two points of data, a general purpose AI agent will likely render your business obsolete within months.

November 13, 2026

  1. The Agent Obsolescence Test

    Before building a new tool, ask if an agent like Meta Muse or GPT-5 could perform the task natively through a chat interface. If the answer is yes, do not build the app. Instead, focus on providing the unique data or specialized hardware access that those agents need to function.

  2. Build Monologue Systems

    As agents begin to shop, schedule, and communicate autonomously, the new bottleneck is trust. There is a growing market for tools that audit and log agent actions. These systems provide a clear paper trail, allowing users to verify what their autonomous assistants did on their behalf.

  3. Transition to Outcome Verification

    The role of the builder is shifting from writing syntax in an IDE to high level planning and auditing. You no longer need to manage the plumbing of the code, but you must become an expert at defining the definition of done. Your primary job is now verifying that the AI output matches the intended business logic.

  4. The Weekly Delegation Habit

    To build intuition for model limits, pick one repetitive chore every week and force an LLM to handle it entirely. This practice moves beyond simple prompting and into building small, durable automations. It reveals exactly where current models fail and where they are ready for production use.

Why it matters

Small businesses often spend months building features that tech giants eventually release as free updates. By shifting focus toward auditing agents and building for agent interfaces, solo builders can create defensive moats that general AI models cannot easily cross.
