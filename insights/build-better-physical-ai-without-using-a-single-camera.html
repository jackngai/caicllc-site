Build Better Physical AI Without Using a Single Camera

High-resolution radar has dropped to a $22 price point, giving builders military-grade sensing capabilities without the privacy baggage of video feeds.

September 17, 2026

  1. Replace PIR with Millimeter Wave

    Passive infrared sensors are binary and unreliable while cameras are intrusive. Using 24GHz radar modules allows your systems to detect micro-movements like breathing to confirm presence even when a person is perfectly still. This is a superior choice for smart office or hospitality applications where privacy is a non-negotiable requirement.

  2. Identify Users via Biometric Signatures

    Every person has a unique gait and cardiac rhythm that radar can capture through walls or clothing. Instead of facial recognition, use these radio-frequency patterns to authenticate users or personalize hardware environments. This creates a friction-free experience where a space reacts to a specific individual without them ever having to look at a lens.

  3. Monitor Internal States via Vibrations

    Neural networks can now classify emotional states based on heart rate variability and subtle chest vibrations captured by radar. You can build systems that adjust notification priority or environmental settings based on a user's stress levels. This shifts AI from reactive command-following to proactive environmental adjustments based on biological data.

  4. Prototype with Low Cost Hardware

    You do not need industrial budgets or specialized clearance to experiment with these capabilities. An ESP32 paired with a 24GHz radar module can replicate sophisticated sensing technology for less than $25. This allows small teams to build high-end smart hardware that was previously restricted to advanced military R&D labs.

Why it matters

Small businesses often face significant liability and trust issues when installing cameras in private or professional spaces. Radar provides rich data on presence, identity, and health without the risk of storing sensitive images. It allows solo builders to ship automation features that feel like magic while maintaining a strict privacy-first architecture.
