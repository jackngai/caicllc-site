Creating Simple Rules for Recurring Tasks

Stop starting from scratch every week by treating your AI as a consistent assistant with its own set of standard operating procedures.

Updated October 18, 2026

Choosing What to Hand Off

Most people use AI for one-off questions, but the real benefit comes from handling the chores you do every week. To find the right tasks to hand off, look for jobs that are low in complexity but happen frequently. These are tasks like summarizing weekly meeting notes, drafting a recurring Friday email to your team, or organizing a list of research links. If you can describe the steps clearly and the task stays mostly the same every time, it is a perfect candidate for the AI.

When you identify these tasks, you move away from being a casual user and start acting like a manager. Instead of hoping the AI gets it right this time, you provide it with a Standard Operating Procedure, often called an SOP. This is simply a set of step-by-step instructions that tells the AI exactly how to handle that specific job. By focusing on these predictable tasks, you build a system where the AI produces reliable results every week without you needing to explain the basics again.

Defining the Assistant's Role

Once you know which task to delegate, you must tell the AI who it is supposed to be for that specific job. In AI terms, this is known as providing context. If you ask for help with an email without any background, the result will feel generic and robotic. Instead, describe the assistant's personality and specific knowledge. Tell it that it is a detail-oriented project coordinator or a warm and professional customer support lead. Provide the specific facts it needs, such as the names of your key projects or the tone your brand uses.

Think of this as an employee handbook for your AI assistant. By defining this identity once, you ensure the tone remains the same across different chat sessions. You are not just typing a prompt; you are building a persona that understands your preferences and your history. When the AI knows its role and the context of your work, it stops guessing and starts acting as a true extension of your own productivity.

Creating a Repeatable Checklist

The most effective way to ensure the AI follows your rules is to turn your own habits into a written checklist. You can do this by recording yourself performing the task one time and typing out the steps as you go. For example, if you are creating a weekly project update, write down the three things you always check: the deadline, the budget status, and the next steps. Feed these steps into the AI and ask it to create a formal checklist for this task.

This checklist becomes a skill that the AI uses whenever you need that job done. Instead of a vague request like "write the report," you tell the AI to use your specific "Weekly Report Checklist." This structure prevents the AI from forgetting small details, like including a specific person on the list or formatting the dates in a certain way. By turning your manual actions into documented steps, you make the process consistent and independent of your own memory.

Managing Your Instruction Library

The final step is to keep these instructions in a central place, such as a simple document or a note-taking app. This acts as a central library for your work. When it is time to perform a recurring task, you copy your instructions and identity details into the chat box. This prevents important details from getting lost in old chat histories. It also means that if you ever need a human colleague to help with the same task, you already have the instructions ready for them as well.

Consistency is the goal of any professional system. By maintaining a library of roles and checklists, you ensure that the AI handles your work exactly the same way every time. You no longer have to worry about the AI making up new rules or ignoring your preferences. With a central set of instructions, you transition from a person who just plays with AI to a person who manages a reliable digital assistant.

Key takeaways

  • Delegate tasks that are low in complexity but happen frequently to ensure the best results from AI.
  • Assign a specific identity and background to the AI so it understands the professional context of your requests.
  • Turn your personal work habits into written checklists or SOPs that the AI can follow step-by-step.
  • Create your SOPs by describing your process once and asking the AI to turn that description into a set of rules.
  • Keep all your roles and instructions in a central document so you can easily reuse them for recurring weekly tasks.
