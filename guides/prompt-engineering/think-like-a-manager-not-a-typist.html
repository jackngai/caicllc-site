Think Like a Manager, Not a Typist

Stop treating AI as a search engine and start treating it as a junior employee by defining the exact steps and standards you expect it to follow.

Updated November 1, 2026

Move Beyond the Quick Answer

When most people use tools like ChatGPT or Claude, they act like a typist asking a librarian for a quick fact. They focus entirely on the product, which is the final email or report they want. This approach often leads to generic or incorrect results because the AI is guessing what you need. To get better results, you must shift your focus from the product to the process. This means telling the AI how to think and what steps to take before it gives you an answer.

A manager does not just say, "Write a client email." A manager explains the goal, the tone, and the specific details that must be included. By describing the process, you guide the AI through a logical path. This reduces the chance of the AI making things up and ensures the final result matches your actual standards. You are no longer just typing questions; you are managing a workflow.

Assign a Role and a Checklist

To make this shift effective, you should treat the AI as a new employee who has zero knowledge of your specific business. Start by giving the AI an identity and context. Tell it who it is supposed to be, such as a detail oriented administrative assistant or a helpful customer service lead. Provide the background info it needs to succeed, such as your company's values or the specific needs of the person you are emailing. This helps the AI stay on track and use the right voice.

Once the identity is set, provide a Standard Operating Procedure, or SOP. An SOP is simply a step by step checklist for the task. If you are asking the AI to summarize a meeting, do not just ask for a summary. Tell it to first list the attendees, then identify the three most important decisions, and finally list the action items with deadlines. You can even create these checklists by recording yourself doing the task and asking the AI to turn your transcript into a set of instructions for future use.

Review the Work with a Critical Eye

Even the best employees need their work checked, and AI is no different. This is where you apply discernment, which is the skill of judging if an output is actually useful and accurate. Instead of trusting the first answer, look for load-bearing facts. These are the specific names, dates, or figures that the rest of the work depends on. If one of these facts is wrong, the entire response might be a failure. Ask the AI to show its reasoning or cite where it found specific information to help you spot these errors.

Another way to check the work is to run a consistency test. If you are unsure about a complex answer, start a fresh chat and give the AI the same instructions again. If the AI provides a completely different logical path or conflicting facts, you know that the task is currently too difficult or your instructions are too vague. High quality management requires this iterative loop of refining your instructions and auditing the results until the output is consistently reliable.

Decide What to Delegate

Not every task should be handed off to an AI. A smart manager uses a simple rule: delegate tasks that are high in reproducibility but low in unique human judgment. For example, if you have a repetitive task like sorting feedback into categories or drafting basic project updates, these are perfect for AI. These tasks follow a clear pattern that you can document in an SOP. This frees up your time for tasks that require deep personal connection or high stakes strategy.

Tasks that require your unique personal voice or involve sensitive human emotions should remain on your desk. By categorizing your work this way, you prevent the quality of your most important work from eroding. You use the AI to handle the work of routine data and drafting, while you focus on the final judgment and the human relationships that the AI cannot replicate. Managing AI is not about replacing your brain; it is about using a system to amplify your productivity.

Key takeaways

  • Focus on the process and steps the AI should follow rather than just the final product.
  • Give the AI a clear identity and provide context about your specific goals and audience.
  • Use checklists or Standard Operating Procedures to define exactly how a task should be completed.
  • Verify load-bearing facts and ask the AI to show its reasoning to catch errors.
  • Delegate repetitive, predictable tasks and keep high-stakes personal judgments for yourself.
  • Test your prompts in new sessions to ensure the AI provides consistent and logical results.
