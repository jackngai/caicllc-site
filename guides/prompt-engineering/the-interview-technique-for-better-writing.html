The Interview Technique for Better Writing

Stop struggling with a robotic tone by instructing the AI to interview you for the details it needs to mimic your personal style.

Updated October 18, 2026

The Problem with Generic Prompts

Most people start using AI by asking it to write an email or a report from scratch. This approach usually fails because the software does not know your specific experiences or your unique way of speaking. When you give the AI a short command, it fills in the blanks with the most common, generic information it can find. This results in writing that sounds like a dry textbook rather than a real person with a unique perspective.

To fix this, you must change how you think about the first step. Instead of trying to write a perfect instruction, start with a raw brain dump. You can record a three minute voice memo on your phone and paste the transcript into the chat box or simply type out a few messy paragraphs of your thoughts. The goal is to get your expertise out of your head and into the tool without worrying about grammar or structure. This raw material acts as the fuel for a better final product.

The Interview Command

Once you have provided your raw notes, do not ask the AI to write the final draft immediately. This is the stage where most users get stuck. Instead, give the AI a specific new role. Tell it to act as a professional interviewer or a ghostwriter. Ask it to read your messy notes and then pose five to ten clarifying questions to fill in any gaps. This forces the tool to look for what is missing in your logic before it attempts to create a finished piece.

When the AI asks you these questions, it acts like a research assistant. It might ask for specific examples of a project you mentioned or how you felt during a particular event. By answering these targeted questions, you are providing the flavor that generic AI lacks. This method ensures the software builds the final draft from your specific answers rather than from a database of boring cliches. You are effectively teaching the AI how you think before it starts writing.

Adding Your Authority

The most effective writing comes from your personal authority. This is especially true if you have years of experience in a specific field. When you answer the AI’s interview questions, use your professional knowledge to provide details that a general computer program would never know. This is how you create content that is resistant to being replaced by generic automation. You are the source of the truth, and the AI is simply the tool that helps you package that truth.

By following this back and forth process, you collapse multiple roles into one conversation. The AI becomes your researcher, your editor, and your strategist all at once. This reduces the time you spend staring at a blank screen or trying to fix a bad first draft. Because the AI spent time interviewing you, the resulting text will reflect your actual expertise and your specific tone. It saves you hours of editing because the first draft already has your voice baked into it.

Scaling Your Efficiency

Once you master this interview technique, you can use it to handle large amounts of work in very little time. Instead of doing this for one email at a time, consider batching your work. This means setting aside one hour to record several voice memos about different topics. You can then feed these transcripts into the AI and run the interview process for all of them in one session. This prevents you from having to restart your creative engine every time you need to write something new.

This habit transforms the AI from a simple chat bot into a sophisticated virtual staff. You spend your energy on the high value task of sharing your ideas while the AI handles the repetitive task of formatting and drafting. The end result is a high quality output that feels authentic to you. You are no longer fighting against a robotic tone because you have provided the soul of the piece through the interview process.

Key takeaways

  • Start with a messy transcript or brain dump instead of trying to write a perfect initial prompt.
  • Explicitly tell the AI to interview you with clarifying questions before it starts the first draft.
  • Use your specific professional experience to answer the questions and provide unique context.
  • Answer the AI's questions to provide the personal style and nuance that generic prompts lack.
  • Batch your ideas by recording multiple thoughts at once to increase your weekly writing speed.
