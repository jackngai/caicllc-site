Managing Risks Using the Door Framework

Learn to distinguish between reversible tasks the AI can handle alone and critical decisions that require your final word.

Updated October 4, 2026

Understanding the Door Framework

To get the best results from AI, you must first understand the risk of the task you are asking it to perform. Think of every task as a door. A two-way door represents a decision that is easy to undo or fix. If you ask an AI to draft a routine email to a colleague and it sounds a bit too formal, you can simply change a few words or ask for a rewrite. The cost of a mistake is low, so you can move fast and rely heavily on the AI to do the initial work.

One-way doors are the opposite. These are decisions that are nearly impossible to reverse once they are made. Examples include making a major career change, signing a long-term contract, or sending a sensitive letter to a landlord. If the AI makes up facts or misses a crucial detail in these scenarios, the damage is significant and permanent. Your success depends on knowing which door you are facing before you start typing into the chat box.

Mastering the Two-Way Doors

For two-way doors, your goal is to build a library of habits. You can treat the AI as a personal assistant that handles the repetitive parts of your day. Start by identifying tasks you do every week, such as organizing rough meeting notes into a list or planning a weekend trip. Instead of starting from scratch every time, give the AI specific feedback on what it gets right and what it gets wrong. Tell it exactly why a certain draft did not work or what details it should prioritize next time.

Over time, this feedback creates a private loop of information that makes the AI more useful to you personally. You are training the tool to understand your unique preferences and the specific details of your life. This turns a generic tool into a personalized skill that you can trigger with a simple command. By delegating these low-risk tasks, you free up your mental energy for the decisions that actually matter. You are not just saving time; you are building a system that handles the small things while you watch the big picture.

Consulting Your AI Focus Group

When you face a one-way door, you must change your approach from delegation to consultation. Do not ask the AI to make the final choice or write the final version. Instead, use it as a sparring partner to test your strategy. You can create an internal focus group by asking the AI to pretend it is a specific person, such as a skeptical boss or a customer with a very tight budget. Ask the AI to interview you about your plan and point out any trade-offs you have ignored or mistakes you might be making.

This method helps you see the hidden risks in a major decision before you commit your name to it. The AI acts as a mirror for your thoughts, allowing you to refine your ideas and prepare for difficult questions. You remain the high-level decision maker, which means you are the one weighing the pros and cons while the AI provides a different perspective. This shift from using AI for tasks to using it for judgment is how you protect yourself from errors and provide real value in your work.

Key takeaways

  • Categorize every AI task as either a reversible two-way door or a high-stakes one-way door.
  • Use AI for speed and drafts on two-way doors like routine emails and summaries.
  • Never let AI make the final call on one-way doors like legal agreements or major life moves.
  • Create an AI focus group by asking the tool to role-play as a person who will critique your work.
  • Improve your results by giving the AI specific feedback on its mistakes to build a personalized skill.
