Teach the AI Your Personal Voice

Stop settling for robotic output by turning the AI into an interviewer that extracts your unique knowledge and writing style.

Updated November 22, 2026

The Interview Method

Most people treat AI like a vending machine. They type a short command and hope a perfect email or report pops out. Because the AI has very little information about who you are or how you speak, it defaults to a safe but boring corporate style. To fix this, you must change the relationship. Instead of giving a direct order, tell the AI to interview you. This simple shift ensures the final result contains your specific ideas and professional authority.

Start by giving the AI a messy brain dump. This could be a rough transcript of a voice memo or three minutes of unedited typing where you explain a project. Command the AI to read your notes and ask five clarifying questions before it writes anything. This process forces the assistant to act as a ghostwriter rather than a template generator. By answering these questions, you provide the missing context that separates a generic response from something that sounds like you.

Define Your Style Boundaries

Once the AI has your ideas, you need to provide rules for how to express them. Every person has specific habits, such as certain words they hate or a specific way they sign off on emails. You can guide the AI by listing these as gotchas. These are hard constraints that tell the assistant exactly what to avoid. If you hate the word leverage or find bullet points too formal for a quick update, tell the AI directly. This prevents the assistant from falling back into its own habits.

Beyond just telling it what to avoid, give it permission to push back. A common mistake is assuming the AI is always right. Tell the assistant to act as a thought partner. If your instructions are unclear or if you are asking it to do something that contradicts your goals, it should point that out. This dynamic stops the AI from being a yes-man and ensures the final text is high quality and accurate.

Organizing Your Knowledge

To keep your results consistent, you should organize your information so the AI knows where to look. Think of this like a digital filing cabinet. You can give the AI specific documents or notes about your business, your past projects, or your common workflows. Instead of re-explaining your background every time you start a new chat, you can refer to these existing records. This saves time and ensures the AI uses your real world experience rather than making up generic details.

As you use these instructions more often, you will notice when they need a tune up. If the AI starts slipping back into robotic language, it means your instructions are getting cluttered. Periodically review your rules and remove anything that is no longer useful. This maintenance keeps the assistant focused on your current voice and goals. This approach works best for topics where you have deep experience, as the AI can then help you scale your output without losing your professional edge.

Key takeaways

  • Ask the AI to interview you before it starts writing to ensure it captures your unique perspective.
  • Provide raw input like voice memo transcripts to give the AI authentic material to work with.
  • List specific phrases or habits as gotchas to prevent the AI from using generic corporate language.
  • Instruct the AI to push back or ask for clarification if your request is vague or inconsistent.
  • Store your common preferences and background info in a central place to avoid repeating yourself in every new chat.
