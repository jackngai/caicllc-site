Keep a Simple List of Project Rules

Stop repeating yourself by keeping a dedicated text file of your preferences, style guides, and past mistakes to paste into every new chat.

Updated September 13, 2026

The Problem with Blank Slates

Every time you start a new chat with an AI, it effectively has amnesia. It does not remember that you prefer concise emails, that you work in the healthcare industry, or that you hate using corporate jargon. While it is tempting to just start typing your request, this leads to a cycle of constant correction. You spend more time fixing the AI's mistakes than if you had simply provided clear instructions from the beginning.

The solution is to stop treating the chat box as the permanent home for your instructions. Instead, create a simple text file or note on your computer titled Project Rules. This file acts as an external brain for your AI interactions. By maintaining this single source of truth, you can copy and paste your core preferences into the first message of any new chat, ensuring the AI starts with the context it needs to be successful.

How to Organize Your Rules

A good rules file is structured so the AI can scan it quickly. Think of it in three layers. The first layer is your Identity, which defines who you are and your general goals. For example, you might state that you are a small business owner who values direct communication. The second layer is your Style Rules, which are hard boundaries for the AI. This includes things like word count limits, specific words to avoid, or a preference for bullet points over long paragraphs.

The third layer is your Skills, which are step-by-step instructions for tasks you do repeatedly. If you write a weekly newsletter, don't just ask for a newsletter. Include a set of steps in your rules file that explains exactly how to draft it: start with a hook, include three links, and end with a call to action. Separating your identity, your style, and your specific processes makes it easier to update the file as your needs change without confusing the AI.

Updating Your Instructions Regularly

Your rules file should not be static. It needs to grow as you learn how the AI behaves. This process is called distillation, where you take the best parts of a successful conversation and turn them into permanent rules. At the end of a particularly helpful chat, ask the AI a simple question: What did you learn about my preferences in this session that I should add to my permanent instructions?

Review the AI's suggestions and add the best ones to your Project Rules file. Conversely, if the AI repeatedly makes a specific mistake despite your rules, that is a sign your instructions are unclear. Refine the wording or add a specific 'Never do this' instruction. By treating every chat as a chance to improve your master document, you ensure that the AI gets smarter and more aligned with your personal style over time.

Preventing Information Overload

As you add more rules, your document can become too long for the AI to process efficiently. This is known as rot, where old or conflicting instructions start to degrade the quality of the AI's output. To prevent this, include a brief summary at the very top of your file that highlights your most important priorities. This allows the AI to understand your main goals immediately before it reads the finer details.

Every few weeks, perform a quick audit of your rules. Delete any instructions that are no longer relevant or that the AI now seems to handle correctly by default. Keeping your rules lean and focused ensures that the AI stays responsive. A short list of high-quality instructions is always more effective than a massive wall of text that the AI might ignore or misinterpret.

Key takeaways

  • Create a 'Project Rules' text file on your computer to store your identity, style preferences, and task steps.
  • Paste your rules into the start of every new chat to avoid the 'blank slate' problem.
  • Ask the AI at the end of a session to summarize new preferences it learned for your rules file.
  • Organize your file with a short summary at the top to help the AI prioritize important information.
  • Prune your rules every few weeks to remove outdated instructions and keep the document sharp.
