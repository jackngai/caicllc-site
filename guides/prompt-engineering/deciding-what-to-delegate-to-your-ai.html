Deciding What to Delegate to Your AI

Stop treating AI like a magic search engine and start treating it like a capable assistant by choosing tasks that follow clear, repeatable patterns.

Updated October 4, 2026

Sorting Your Tasks by Predictability

The first step to getting better results is realizing that AI is not a mind reader but a pattern matcher. It performs best on tasks that are high in reproducibility, which means they follow a similar set of steps every time you do them. Think about drafting a standard weekly update email or summarizing a long article. These are chores where the logic stays the same even if the specific details change. When a task is predictable, the AI can handle the heavy lifting while you focus on the final polish.

Conversely, you should avoid delegating tasks that require high-stakes human judgment or your unique personal voice. If a mistake in a task could cause a major problem or if the work requires a level of empathy only you can provide, keep it on your own desk. Think of the AI as a junior assistant who is great at following a manual but lacks the life experience to make big decisions. By filtering your daily chores this way, you ensure the AI works on the 'autopilot' tasks while you save your energy for the work that truly matters.

Building a Better Job Description

Once you have picked a predictable task, you need to provide the AI with a clear identity and a set of instructions. This is more than just a quick question. You should define the AI's role for that specific conversation. For example, if you are planning a trip, tell the AI to act like a travel agent who specializes in budget family vacations. This identity gives the AI a specific lens to look through, which prevents it from giving you generic or useless advice.

To make the results even more reliable, give the AI a Standard Operating Procedure, or SOP. An SOP is simply a step-by-step checklist of how you want the task finished. A great way to create one is to record yourself talking through a task as you do it, then paste that transcript into the AI and ask it to write a checklist for that role. When the AI has both a clear identity and a checklist of steps, it moves from just guessing to actually performing a skill for you.

The Habit of Checking the Work

Even with clear instructions, AI can sometimes make things up. This is often called a hallucination. To prevent this from ruining your work, you must use a discernment loop. This is a habit of auditing the AI’s output before you use it. Focus on checking the load-bearing facts. These are the specific names, dates, or numbers that must be correct for the whole piece of work to be valid. If those facts are wrong, the entire response falls apart. Ask the AI to show its reasoning so you can see exactly how it arrived at its conclusion.

You can also test the logic of your instructions by running the same prompt in a brand new chat window. If the AI gives you a completely different answer the second time, it is a sign that your instructions are too vague or the task is too speculative for the model to handle reliably. Use these differences to sharpen your checklist. By consistently checking the results and refining your process, you turn the AI from an unpredictable toy into a dependable part of your daily workflow.

Key takeaways

  • Prioritize tasks that are highly repeatable and follow a clear set of steps.
  • Define a specific identity for the AI to ensure it uses the right tone and context.
  • Turn your own habits into checklists by using transcripts to create AI instructions.
  • Verify load-bearing facts like dates and names to catch potential hallucinations.
  • Run the same prompt twice in separate chats to check for logic consistency.
