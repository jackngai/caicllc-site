Decide What to Do Yourself versus What to Delegate

Master the art of splitting your workload by identifying which tasks require your human judgment and which can be offloaded to AI for faster results.

Updated September 13, 2026

Sorting Your To-Do List

To get better results from AI, you must stop treating it like a search engine and start treating it like a capable assistant. The first step is looking at your daily tasks through two lenses: how complex is the task, and how often does it repeat? Tasks that are low in complexity and happen the same way every time (such as summarizing meeting notes or formatting a basic email list) are perfect candidates for delegation. These are your reproducible tasks where the steps stay constant.

On the other hand, tasks that require high levels of personal judgment or involve high stakes should remain on your desk. Think of this as your keep list. If a task requires your specific personality, deep empathy, or a high-level strategy that changes every time, you should lead the work while using the AI only for minor support. This distinction prevents you from delegating your most valuable thinking to a machine that cannot truly feel or reason.

Managing Risks with One-Way Doors

Once you identify a task to delegate, you need to weigh the risk of a mistake. Think of your choices as either one-way or two-way doors. A two-way door is a decision that is easily reversed, such as drafting a routine internal memo or brainstorming ideas for a team lunch. If the AI gets it wrong, you can simply fix it or try again with no lasting damage. These are the areas where you can give the AI the most freedom to work on its own.

A one-way door is a decision that is difficult or impossible to undo, like sending a final price quote to a major client or publishing a sensitive public statement. For these high-stakes moments, the AI should only assist you in the drafting phase. You must remain the final gatekeeper because you are the one responsible for the outcome. By identifying these doors early, you avoid the trap of trusting the AI with decisions that only a human should make.

Defining the Process, Not Just the Product

When you hand a task over, most people only describe the product they want, like asking for a report on local house prices. To get professional results, you must instead describe the process or how the AI should think. Tell the AI what its role is (for example, "You are an expert researcher with a focus on accuracy") and provide it with specific background information. This context acts like a handbook for your new digital assistant, ensuring it follows your logic rather than making its own guesses.

Providing this context allows you to build skills for the AI. If you find yourself giving the same instructions every week, write them down as a set of standard steps. You can even record yourself doing the task and have the AI turn that recording into a checklist for itself. This shifts your role from a person who types prompts to a manager who designs the workflows that the AI follows to stay consistent.

Closing the Discernment Loop

Delegation does not mean you can set it and forget it. Even the best AI can experience hallucinations, which are moments where it confidently states something that is factually incorrect. To manage this, you need a discernment loop. This is a quick check where you ask the AI to show its work or explain the reasoning behind its answer. If you are unsure about a fact, ask the AI to cite its source or try running the same prompt in a fresh chat window to see if the answer remains consistent.

By verifying the most important parts of the output, you build a relationship of trust and verification. This final step completes the delegation cycle. You have sorted the task, weighed the risk, defined the process, and verified the result. Over time, this habit turns your chat box into a powerful extension of your own capabilities, allowing you to focus on the high-level decisions that truly make a difference in your work.

Key takeaways

  • Use a matrix to find tasks that are low in complexity but repeat often; these are your best targets for AI.
  • Identify one-way door decisions that are high-stakes and irreversible to ensure you keep human control over them.
  • Build a keep list of tasks involving unique personal voice or strategy that the AI should not handle alone.
  • Describe the process of how the AI should think rather than just the final product you want it to create.
  • Use a discernment loop to check the AI's reasoning and verify important facts to catch potential errors.
