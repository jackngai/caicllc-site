Use the Interview Method for Better Context

Instead of guessing what information the AI needs, instruct the tool to interview you so it can gather the specific details required for a high-quality result.

Updated September 13, 2026

Stop Guessing and Start Delegating

Most people approach AI by trying to write the perfect, all-in-one prompt. They spend several minutes typing out every detail they think might be relevant, only to receive a generic result that misses the mark. This happens because humans are often poor judges of what a computer needs to know to perform a task well. When you try to provide all the context upfront, you are essentially guessing what the AI’s blind spots are.

A better way to work is to treat the AI like a capable new assistant. If you hired a person to help you plan a company retreat or write a monthly newsletter, you would not expect them to know your preferences immediately. You would expect them to ask questions. You can shift this responsibility to the AI by asking it to take the lead in the discovery process. This ensures the information provided is exactly what the model needs to succeed.

How to Trigger the Interview

To start this process, give the AI a clear goal followed by a request to be questioned. For example, you might say, I want you to write a series of emails for my landscaping business. Before you start, I want you to interview me. Ask me five specific questions about my services, my tone of voice, and my target audience so you have all the context you need. This forces the AI to act as a supervisor that defines the boundaries of the task before it attempts any work.

This method is even more effective if you provide a raw dump of your thoughts to start. You can paste a rough transcript of a voice note or a messy list of bullet points and tell the AI to grill you on the gaps. Instead of you struggling to structure your ideas, the AI looks at your raw input and identifies what is missing. By answering these targeted questions, you provide high-quality data that results in a much more personalized and professional output.

Confirm the Plan Before the Work

Once the interview is over, do not let the AI jump straight into the final task. Instead, ask it to summarize its understanding of the project into a short plan or a recipe card. This is a simple document that lists the goals, the intended tone, and the specific steps the AI will take to finish the job. Reviewing this plan acts as a checkpoint. It allows you to catch misunderstandings early before the AI spends time generating pages of text that you might have to throw away.

Think of this as an intent statement. By confirming the plan first, you create a shared source of truth for the conversation. If the plan looks good, you can give the AI the green light to proceed. If it feels off, you can correct the course by saying, the plan is mostly right, but make the tone more professional and less salesy. This small step prevents the common frustration of receiving a long document that is fundamentally wrong because of a simple misunderstanding at the start.

The Critique and Polish Loop

After the AI provides a draft based on your interview and plan, you can use the same questioning logic to polish the result. If the draft is not perfect, do not just tell it to try again. Ask the AI to critique its own work or ask you for one more round of details to fix the weak spots. This creates a loop where the AI acts as both the worker and the quality control manager, reducing the mental load on you to find every error.

By following this sequence, you move from being a prompt writer to being a decision maker. You are no longer responsible for the tedious work of structuring the information or guessing the requirements. Instead, you focus on providing the raw expertise that only you have, while the AI handles the organization and execution. This method turns the chat box from a simple search tool into a collaborative partner that learns your unique voice and needs.

Key takeaways

  • Instruct the AI to interview you with specific questions before it starts any major task.
  • Paste raw thoughts or messy notes and ask the AI to identify what context is missing.
  • Always require the AI to present a plan or summary of intent before it generates a final draft.
  • Use a multi step process where the AI first gathers info, then plans, and then executes.
  • Shift your role from a writer trying to be perfect to a manager who reviews and approves.
