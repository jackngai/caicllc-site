The Double Check Habit for Finding Errors

Learn how to spot AI mistakes before they cause problems by making the model show its work and auditing the facts that support its logic.

Updated October 18, 2026

The Problem with Silent Mistakes

AI models are designed to be helpful and confident, even when they are wrong. These errors are often called hallucinations. They happen because the AI is predicting the next word rather than checking a database of facts. If you ask it to write an email about a specific policy, it might make up a detail that sounds perfectly plausible but is completely false.

To avoid being misled, you must move from simply accepting the final product to inspecting the process. This means looking at how the AI reached its conclusion rather than just reading the final result. By treating the AI like an assistant who needs a second look, you prevent small errors from becoming big problems in your daily work.

Identifying Load Bearing Facts

When you review an AI output, focus your energy on the load bearing facts. These are the specific pieces of information that, if wrong, would make the entire message fail. For example, in a travel itinerary, the flight times and hotel names are load bearing. In a summary of a meeting, the names of the people assigned to tasks are the critical parts.

Ask the AI to list the specific facts it used to generate its response. By separating the raw data from the prose, you can quickly verify whether the foundation of the answer is solid. If the AI cannot point to a source or a clear reason for a specific detail, that is a red flag that it might be making things up.

Forcing the AI to Show Its Work

One of the most effective ways to catch errors is to ask the AI to think out loud. Instead of just asking for a finished draft of a project plan, tell the AI to explain its reasoning step by step before it gives you the final answer. This forces the model to follow a logical path, which often prevents it from jumping to a wrong conclusion.

This discernment loop acts like an internal audit. When you see the steps the AI took, you can spot exactly where the logic went off the rails. If the reasoning for a specific budget recommendation looks shaky, you know to ignore the final number and provide more context to steer the AI back on track.

The Consistency Test

If you are unsure about an answer, run a consistency test. Start a completely new chat session and give the AI the same prompt again. Because AI is based on probabilities, it might approach the problem differently the second time. If the two answers agree on the core details, your confidence in the result should increase.

However, if the two sessions produce wildly different numbers or conflicting advice, you have identified a weak point in the logic. These discrepancies show you exactly where the AI is guessing rather than knowing. This habit ensures that you only use AI for tasks where it can provide stable and reliable help, keeping the high stakes decisions under your own control.

Key takeaways

  • Always identify the load bearing facts in any AI response and verify them against your original source.
  • Ask the AI to explain its reasoning step by step to expose logic errors before the final draft is written.
  • Use a consistency test by running the same request in a fresh chat window to see if the answers match.
  • Treat the AI as an assistant whose work always requires a final human audit before it is sent or used.
  • Focus your energy on inspecting the process the AI used rather than just the product it delivered.
