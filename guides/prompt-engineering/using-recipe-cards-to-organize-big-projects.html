Using Recipe Cards to Organize Big Projects

Learn how to manage complex tasks by breaking them into smaller steps and requiring the AI to pause for your approval at every milestone.

Updated November 1, 2026

The Problem with Big Requests

When you ask an AI to handle a large project like planning a cross-country move or drafting a business plan, the results often feel generic or messy. This happens because the AI tries to finish the entire task in one go. It makes assumptions about what you want and fills in the gaps with its own guesses. Instead of treating the AI as a magic button that produces a finished product, you should treat it as a capable assistant that needs a clear plan of action.

To get better results, you must first deconstruct your goal into its smallest parts. Think of this as creating a Recipe Card. A Recipe Card is a written list of every sub-task required to reach your goal. For example, if you are planning a company offsite, your recipe would include researching locations, comparing costs, drafting an invite, and building an itinerary. By separating the work into these tiny chunks, you prevent the AI from feeling overwhelmed and ensure it stays focused on one detail at a time.

Setting Up the Manager Loop

Once you have your Recipe Card, you need to change how you talk to the AI. Instead of simply asking it to do the work, tell the AI to act as a Project Manager. Give it your list of steps and instruct it to follow them in order. The most important rule to set is the approval checkpoint. Tell the AI: "Do not move to the next step until I have reviewed and approved the current one." This creates what is known as a Manager Loop, where you stay in control of the direction.

This method stops the AI from wandering off track. If the AI is researching hotels for your event and picks options that are too expensive, you can correct it immediately. Without this loop, the AI might spend several paragraphs planning activities based on a hotel you don't even like. By forcing these pauses, you ensure that the final result is exactly what you need rather than a guess that requires hours of editing.

Assigning a Clear Mission

To make your Recipe Card even more effective, use a simple framework to define the AI's role. Before the AI starts the first step, tell it exactly who it is and what it is trying to achieve. Professional builders call this the AIM framework: Actor, Input, and Mission. For a household budget project, you might say: "You are a frugal financial advisor (Actor). I am providing my last three months of bank statements (Input). Your mission is to identify three areas where I can save $200 a month (Mission)."

Applying this framework keeps the AI from giving you generic advice you could find anywhere on the internet. It forces the AI to use your specific data and adopt a specific mindset. When you combine this clear identity with your step-by-step Recipe Card, the AI stops being a simple chatbot and starts acting like a specialized teammate. You provide the high-level strategy and the final approvals, while the AI handles the time-consuming research and drafting.

Key takeaways

  • Break every large goal into a list of tiny, manageable sub-tasks before you start.
  • Tell the AI to act as a Project Manager and follow your list one step at a time.
  • Require the AI to ask for your explicit approval before it moves from one step to the next.
  • Define the Actor, Input, and Mission for every project to avoid generic responses.
  • Focus your energy on reviewing the AI's work and making decisions rather than doing the manual labor yourself.
