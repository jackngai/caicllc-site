Attach Images to Describe What You See

Stop struggling to find the right words and start using visuals to give your AI assistant perfect context for every task.

Updated September 13, 2026

Give Your AI a Pair of Eyes

Most people treat AI like a blind assistant. They spend minutes typing out long descriptions of what they are looking at, whether it is a confusing chart or a cluttered inbox. Modern tools have multimodal features, which simply means they can see and understand images. By attaching a screenshot, you provide an instant map of your problem that words alone cannot match. This allows the AI to ground its answers in reality rather than guessing based on your text.

This approach works best when you need the AI to analyze something specific. If you have a physical document, a photo of a whiteboard, or a screenshot of a confusing website, attach it first. Tell the AI what to look for within that image. Instead of saying there is a button at the top left, just point to the image and ask what the button does. This reduces the chance of the AI hallucinating or making up details that are not there.

Recording the Action

Building on the power of still images, you can now use motion to explain complex situations. If you are trying to show how a specific app is acting up or how a website menu moves, you can record a short screen video. Advanced models can now process video files by breaking them down into individual frames. This allows the AI to see the sequence of events and understand the flow of what you are doing in real time.

When you upload a video, the AI acts like a high speed observer. It looks at every frame and can even listen to the audio if you are speaking. This is incredibly useful for creating instructions. You can record yourself doing a task once, upload the video, and ask the AI to write a step by step guide based on your actions. It saves you from having to type out every single click and movement.

Practical Habits for Better Results

To get the most out of these visual tools, keep your files clean and focused. If a video is too long or a screenshot is too cluttered, the AI might get distracted by unimportant details. For videos longer than fifteen minutes, try lowering the resolution so the file is easier for the AI to read. For screenshots, crop the image so only the relevant part of the screen is visible. This helps the AI focus its attention on the specific issue at hand.

You can also use mobile apps to bridge the gap between the physical and digital worlds. Many AI apps allow you to share your screen live or use your camera to ask questions about your surroundings. If you are stuck on a home repair or trying to organize a shelf, point your camera and talk to the AI in real time. It can give you advice based on exactly what it sees, turning your phone into a collaborative tool for everyday life. By showing rather than just telling, you ensure the AI sees exactly what you see.

Key takeaways

  • Use screenshots to provide instant context for complex layouts or documents.
  • Record short screen videos to help the AI understand movement or multi-step processes.
  • Crop your images to keep the AI focused on the most important details.
  • Lower the resolution of long videos to ensure the AI can process the entire file efficiently.
  • Use mobile screen-sharing features to get real-time help with apps and navigation.
  • Always provide a text instruction alongside your image to tell the AI exactly what to analyze.
